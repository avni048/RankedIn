How matching works

Keyword matching: resume and JD text are scanned against a curated skill dictionary with an alias table (e.g. "JS" → "JavaScript", "Postgres" → "PostgreSQL"). Required-skill coverage and preferred-skill coverage are each computed as matched ÷ total.

Semantic matching: the JD's requirement/responsibility sentences and the resume's sentences are vectorized with TF-IDF and compared with cosine similarity, taking the best-matching resume sentence per JD requirement, then averaging. This is why "built backend services with Express and MongoDB" scores well against "develop REST APIs using Node.js and MongoDB" even without an exact word match — they share enough context vocabulary. A small, capped bonus is also added when a required skill is missing literally but a curated ontology shows a directly related technology is present (e.g. Express relates to Node.js) — this bonus and the reasoning behind it is always shown in candidate evidence, never hidden.

Experience & project relevance: the Experience and Projects sections of the resume are checked separately for required-skill (or related-skill) mentions, so skills only listed in a generic skills list score differently from skills demonstrated in actual work.

Final score = weighted sum of the five components above, each normalized to 0–100.

Tie-breaking: sort by final score, then by required-skill score, then alphabetically by candidate name — fully deterministic.
